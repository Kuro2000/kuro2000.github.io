Cat Dash

Amanotes is a leading music-game publisher with 3B+ downloads worldwide. My team’s job was converting hyper-casual hits into long-term hybrid products. Cat Dash is my strongest product-thinking story.

The bet

Cat Dash started life as a hyper-casual platformer where a cat runs to the beat. Fun for a session, built to be disposable. The conversion brief: give it the systems of a game people return to for months, without breaking the two-taps-and-you’re-playing simplicity that made it work.

An economy from scratch

The game had no economy, so I built one from the ground up.

  • Defined the monetizable item set: skins, songs, hearts, coins, boosters
  • Mapped every source and sink so resources flow toward long-term IAP rather than one-off spikes
  • Converted the ad-gated shop into a coin-based player shop, turning ad views into a currency loop

The short-levels hypothesis

Our levels ran long for how people actually play on phones. I hypothesized that shorter levels would fit modern play sessions better, using the Geometry Dash to Geometry Dash World restructuring as precedent. Then I designed the A/B test, ran it, and the data agreed. We rolled the restructured levels out to everyone.

Hypothesis, test, rollout. Not taste, not seniority, not the loudest voice in the room. This loop is how I want every design argument settled.

Redesigning the level journey

With shorter levels confirmed, I rebuilt the journey around player feeling instead of raw challenge: an easy hook to open, difficulty rising on a sawtooth curve so relief follows every spike, and pacing structured with the Kishōtenketsu method borrowed from Japanese narrative design.

Recurring monetization

  • Designed the Battle Pass with exclusive song-pack Chapters, giving the music itself a progression spine
  • Balanced free and premium tracks so paying feels like a boost, not a toll

Outcome

Cat Dash was the only new product of its cohort to pass both the prototype gate and the soft-launch gate in 2025.

The sibling story matters too. On Tiles Master, a music-PvP spin-off I designed the core loop, bot logic, and dynamic matchmaking for, the numbers said no: strong CPI in testing, but D7 retention below the bar. We killed it. Knowing when the data says stop is the same skill as knowing when it says go.
